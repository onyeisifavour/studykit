## INPUT: THE TOPIC LIST FILE

You will be presented with ONE subject's section at a time (for example, only
the Maths section, or only the Physics section). Within that section, the
subject and its topics are divided by markdown headers:

- **Subject** header: `## **Maths**` (one subject per run; other subjects are
  given to you in separate runs)
- **Topic** headers: `#### L1.1 <Topic Name>` (all topics of that subject)
- **Skill scope** headers: `##### Scope Concept` (or `##### Scope Concept(s)`) —
  a numbered list of the exact skills/concepts each topic must cover

Formulas usually appear as clean LaTeX. If any are rendered as inline base64
images or garbled fragments (from an Obsidian export), ignore those and work
from the `Scope Concept` skill list and any readable LaTeX. Re-express every
formula you use as clean LaTeX in your output.

## QUANTITY & COVERAGE (per topic)

Generate exactly **30 questions per topic**:

- **25 MCQ/Hybrid questions** (any mix of MCQ and Hybrid you judge best for the
  skills involved)
- **5 Theory questions** (NO constraint on the number of sub-parts — mirror the
  real past questions you found; some should run deep, a, b, c, d, e, ...)

Coverage rule: every skill listed in the topic's `Scope Concept` must be tested
at least once across that topic's 30 questions. Because a topic can list more
than 30 skills, one question may (and often should) test several related skills.
The union of skills tested by the 30 questions must equal the full scope list —
nothing in the scope may be left out.

## WEB RESEARCH & QUESTION SOURCING (deep, sequential — do not skim)

You have live web-search capability. USE IT on EVERY active topic block, going
skill by skill through that topic's `Scope Concept`. This is a deep, sequential
pass — never a skim.

1. **Query per skill.** For EACH skill in the topic's `Scope Concept`, run
   targeted searches against live past-exam databases, e.g.
   `<skill phrase> past exam questions [board]` — repeating for NECO, WAEC,
   Cambridge IGCSE, IB, AP, JAMB, A-Level, and equivalent boards. Vary the
   query ("worked example", "mark scheme", "exam question", "solved") until
   you land on genuine official material. Do not move to the next skill until
   you have source material for this one.
2. **Extract the style.** From the best 2-3 sources per skill, record the
   official question phrasing, difficulty, expected answer depth, and the
   traps the board sets. This is what you must replicate.
3. **Adapt, rephrase, or replicate.** Turn the best real questions into your
   bank:
   - Replicate the exact style, rigor, and trickiness of the official board:
     same question shape, same demand for working, same
     distractors-as-common-mistakes for MCQ.
   - Rephrase scenarios and data so each is a close adaptation, not a verbatim
     copy. Combine ideas from several real questions where the scope requires.
   - Source REAL STRUCTURED/ESSAY theory questions too, not only objective
     ones. When a genuine past theory question is found, capture its exact
     layering — how its sub-parts (a), (b), (c) build from a first task to
     harder ones, and how much each demands. Use that question (or a close
     adaptation of it) in the topic's Theory section.
4. **Tag exactly** on every question:
   - Adapted from a genuine past question: `Exam: <exact board name>`
     (e.g. `Exam: WAEC 2019`, `Exam: Cambridge IGCSE 0580/22`). State the
     board you actually used.
   - Formulated completely from scratch: `Exam: null`. Never attach a board
     name to a question you invented.
5. **Verify before finalising.** Confirm every adapted answer against its
   source, or work it independently. Finalise a question only when you are
   certain its answer and working are correct.
6. **No skimming.** Every skill gets a genuine search pass and at least one
   source-informed question. Never generate a topic purely from your own head
   when real board material exists for it.

## QUESTION PROVENANCE & THE EXAM TAG

Every question must carry an Exam tag, but its value depends on where the

- **Formulated / original** (written by you for this bank): `Exam: null`
- **Taken or adapted from a real past exam question** found via web research

Prefer sourcing real past questions over writing from scratch — real questions

## OUTPUT FORMAT (Do NOT deviate from this structure)

Format your output as markdown. Each topic gets its own block. Question
numbering restarts at 1 within every topic block (continuous across its MCQ /
Hybrid / Theory sections), so each block can be copied into its own file
as-is.

Each question ends with tags in square brackets:
[Type: value; Subject: value; Topic: value; Format: value; Exam: value]

Available tag values:
- Type:
  - MCQ — multiple choice: a stem plus exactly 4 options (A, B, C, D); the
    student picks the one correct option.
  - Hybrid — objective, no options: a short-answer question with one definite
    correct answer (a number, value, expression, or short conclusion) the
    student computes and writes down; requires real working but no essay-style
    multi-part response.
  - Theory — structured: a multi-part question with any number of sub-parts
    (a), (b), (c), (d), ... demanding proofs, derivations, or explained
    reasoning.
- Subject: the subject header the topic belongs to (e.g., Maths, Physics, Chem)
- Topic: the exact `####` topic header name
- Format: Non-Sim (all questions are non-simulation)
- Exam: null (formulated) OR the exam board (adapted past question)

```
# SUBJECT: [SUBJECT NAME]

## TOPIC: [TOPIC NAME]

### MCQ QUESTIONS

1. Question one... [Type: MCQ; ...]
2. Question two... [Type: MCQ; ...]
...

### HYBRID QUESTIONS

<numbering continues> ... [Type: Hybrid; ...]
...

### THEORY QUESTIONS

<numbering continues> ... [Type: Theory; ...]
...

### ANSWER BANK

1. Answer one... [Type: MCQ]
2. Answer two... [Type: ...]
...
30. Answer thirty... [Type: Theory]
```

Numbering runs CONTINUOUSLY across the three question sections (1-30 per
topic) — it never restarts at a section boundary. The section headers
(`### MCQ QUESTIONS`, `### HYBRID QUESTIONS`, `### THEORY QUESTIONS`) only
group the questions by type for the reader. The app pairs each question with
the answer of the SAME number, so the ANSWER BANK holds all 30 answers,
numbered 1-30 to match.

## INSTRUCTIONS FOR QUESTION GENERATION

Every question you generate must carry the full tag set:
[Type: ...; Subject: ...; Topic: ...; Format: Non-Sim; Exam: null-or-board]

### MCQ Questions:

1. **Structure:** Question stem followed by 4 options (A, B, C, D)
2. **One correct answer:** Exactly one option is correct
3. **Plausible distractors:** Wrong options should be common mistakes
4. **Tag:** [Type: MCQ; Subject: ...; Topic: ...; Format: Non-Sim; Exam: ...]

### Hybrid Questions (Objective, no options):

A Hybrid is a short-answer question with NO answer options. It has one definite
correct answer — a number, value, expression, unit, or short conclusion — which
the student works out and writes down. It is the middle type:
- Like MCQ: one right answer, no essay-style response.
- Like Theory: real working is required, often multi-step, and may include a
  justify-your-answer demand.
Rule of thumb: no options + one definite short answer = Hybrid. If a question
would read awkwardly with A-D options attached, make it a Hybrid.

1. **Foundation questions:** Basic recall + single-step calculation
2. **Intermediate questions:** Multi-step problem-solving, conversions, applications
3. **Synthesis questions:** Require proof, counterexample, or deep reasoning
4. **Each question MUST:**
   - Have a clear, measurable answer
   - Require showing working
   - Progress logically in difficulty
   - Test understanding, not rote memorization
   - Test one of the skills in its topic's Scope Concept
5. **Tag:** [Type: Hybrid; Subject: ...; Topic: ...; Format: Non-Sim; Exam: ...]

### Theory Questions (Structured, exam-authentic):

Model each theory question on elite exam-paper style, like the example in
folder X (topicfolder1): a bold titled question with a realistic context and
natural sub-tasks — never a forced teaching ladder, and NO constraint on how
many sub-parts a question may carry. Real exam theory questions go deep
(a, b, c, d, e, ...); match that depth and make theory the weightiest part of
the bank.

1. **Title line:** Open with a bold title that names the task, e.g.
   `**1. Base Conversion via Multiple Pathways**`
2. **Source-driven:** Prefer adapting a real past-exam theory question you
   found in research (tag its board). Only formulate your own when no suitable
   real question exists.
3. **Mirror the real layering:** Whatever theory you produce, match the
   sub-part layering of the real past questions found during research — their
   depth, their progression from an opening task to harder parts, and how many
   sub-parts they use. Do not force a fixed template; let the genuine question
   structures you gathered drive the shape of both adapted and formulated
   theory questions.
4. **Realistic stem (optional):** When it helps, set the question in a real
   context or hand over the working data, e.g. "A digital sensor outputs the
   binary string 110110101₂." or an equation with its variables defined. A
   bare instruction is fine for pure proofs.
5. **Sub-parts (a), (b), (c), (d), ... — unlimited count:** Build each
   question from however many sub-parts it genuinely needs, reading like real
   exam tasks. Layer them naturally: an opening task, then verification or
   extension by alternate pathways, then conceptual explanation or practical
   implication, and anything further the question demands. Match the stress
   and depth of real board theory questions — longer and more layered is
   right, not shorter.
6. **Content demands:** each topic must include at least one theory question
   requiring a formal proof or a disproof-by-counterexample, and at least one
   requiring a conceptual explanation with mathematical support.
7. **Tag:** [Type: Theory; Subject: ...; Topic: ...; Format: Non-Sim; Exam: ...]

### Answer Bank Rules:

1. **Full working:** Show every algebraic step, not just final answers
2. **Explicit reasoning:** Explain *why* each step follows logically
3. **Alternative methods:** If applicable, show alternate valid approaches
4. **Theory answers mirror the question:** answer every sub-part in order with
   full working, exactly matching the question's title and structure.
5. **Tag:** Every answer must end with [Type: MCQ], [Type: Hybrid], or [Type: Theory] matching its question (answers carry only the Type tag)
6. **LaTeX:** Answers must also use LaTeX for any formulas
