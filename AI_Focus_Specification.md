# AI Focus Specification

## 1. Title
**Gravitational Fields and Orbital Mechanics: Interactive Learning Laboratory**

## 2. Subject Area
Physics (Classical Mechanics)

## 3. Topic Focus
Gravitational Fields and Orbital Mechanics: Understanding gravitational force variation with distance, orbital speed-gravity balance, and interpretation of orbital parameters through interactive simulation.

## 4. Target Audience
- **Primary:** High school physics students (grades 11-12) and introductory university physics students
- **Secondary:** Self-learners with basic physics knowledge, educators seeking interactive teaching tools
- **Prerequisites:** Basic understanding of Newton's laws of motion, concept of force, and basic algebra

## 5. Learning Objectives
Upon completion, learners will be able to:
1. **Demonstrate** how gravitational force varies with distance according to the inverse-square law
2. **Explain** how orbital speed balances gravitational force to maintain stable orbits
3. **Interpret** orbital parameters (semi-major axis, eccentricity, period, velocity) from simulation data
4. **Predict** changes in orbital characteristics when initial conditions are modified
5. **Analyze** the relationship between orbital radius and orbital period
6. **Evaluate** the stability of different orbital configurations

## 6. Concept Decomposition
### Core Concepts:
1. **Gravitational Force**
   - Newton's law of universal gravitation
   - Inverse-square relationship
   - Force vectors and direction
   - Gravitational field strength

2. **Orbital Mechanics**
   - Circular vs. elliptical orbits
   - Orbital speed and velocity vectors
   - Centripetal acceleration
   - Conservation of energy in orbits

3. **Orbital Parameters**
   - Semi-major axis
   - Eccentricity
   - Orbital period
   - Apoapsis and periapsis
   - Velocity at different orbital positions

4. **Gravitational Interactions**
   - Two-body problem
   - Gravitational potential energy
   - Escape velocity
   - Gravitational assists (conceptual)

## 7. Conceptual Dependencies
### Prerequisite Knowledge:
1. **Newton's First Law** (inertia)
2. **Newton's Second Law** (F=ma)
3. **Newton's Third Law** (action-reaction)
4. **Basic vector mathematics** (addition, decomposition)
5. **Concept of work and energy**
6. **Basic algebra and proportional reasoning**

### Concept Relationships:
- Gravitational force provides centripetal force for circular orbits
- Orbital speed determines orbit shape and size
- Energy conservation links orbital speed and position
- Gravitational field strength determines orbital characteristics

## 8. Instructional Sequence
### Phase 1: Foundation Building
1. **Introduction to gravitational fields** - Visualize field lines and strength variation

3. **Vector representation** - Understanding force direction and magnitude

### Phase 2: Orbital Dynamics
4. **Circular orbit equilibrium** - Balancing gravitational and centripetal forces
5. **Orbital speed calculation** - Relating speed to orbital radius
6. **Energy in orbits** - Kinetic and potential energy trade-offs

### Phase 3: Complex Orbits
7. **Elliptical orbits** - Introducing eccentricity and orbital shapes
8. **Orbital parameters** - Interpreting Kepler's laws through simulation
9. **Orbit modification** - Changing initial conditions to alter orbits

### Phase 4: Synthesis
10. **Multi-body scenarios** - Introducing additional gravitational influences
11. **Escape velocity** - Breaking free from gravitational binding
12. **Real-world applications** - Satellites, planets, and space missions

## 9. Misconception Analysis
### Common Misconceptions:
1. **"Gravity disappears in space"**
   - **Reality:** Gravity acts over infinite distances, though weakens with distance
   - **Address:** Show gravitational field lines extending infinitely with decreasing density

2. **"Orbits require constant forward thrust"**
   - **Reality:** Orbits are stable due to inertia balancing gravity
   - **Address:** Demonstrate orbit maintenance without continuous thrust

3. **"Heavier objects fall faster"**
   - **Reality:** Gravitational acceleration is independent of mass (in vacuum)
   - **Address:** Show objects of different masses orbiting identically

4. **"Orbits are perfect circles"**
   - **Reality:** Most orbits are elliptical with varying eccentricity
   - **Address:** Allow manipulation of eccentricity to see orbit shape changes

5. **"Gravity acts only downward"**
   - **Reality:** Gravity always acts toward the center of mass
   - **Address:** Show force vectors pointing toward central body from all positions

## 10. Cognitive Load Strategy
### Progressive Complexity:
1. **Start with circular orbits** - Simplest case, clear force balance
2. **Introduce one variable at a time** - First radius, then speed, then mass
3. **Visual scaffolding** - Begin with explicit vectors, gradually reduce visual cues
4. **Chunked information** - Present orbital parameters in logical groups
5. **Interactive pacing** - Learner controls simulation speed and complexity

### Manageable Information:
- **Initial view:** Show only essential elements (central body, orbiting object, force vector)
- **Progressive disclosure:** Add parameters, vectors, and data as needed
- **Guided attention:** Highlight relevant relationships during key moments
- **Summary panels:** Consolidate information after exploration phases

## 11. Knowledge Representation
### Visualization Approach:
1. **Force vectors** - Color-coded arrows showing magnitude and direction
2. **Orbital paths** - Clear trajectory lines with position markers
3. **Field lines** - Gravitational field strength visualization
4. **Data readouts** - Real-time display of velocity, acceleration, force, energy
5. **Parameter controls** - Interactive sliders for key variables
6. **Comparison views** - Side-by-side orbits for contrast

### Representation Modes:
- **Spatial:** 3D visualization of orbits and fields
- **Numerical:** Real-time data displays and calculations
- **Symbolic:** Equations linking variables
- **Graphical:** Relationships between parameters (e.g., radius vs. period)

## 12. Assessment Strategy
### Formative Assessment:
1. **Exploration checkpoints** - Prompted questions during simulation
2. **Prediction tasks** - "What will happen if...?" before manipulation
3. **Parameter identification** - Identify orbital characteristics from visualization
4. **Error analysis** - Diagnose why orbits become unstable

### Summative Assessment:
1. **Orbit design challenges** - Create specific orbital configurations
2. **Parameter interpretation** - Read and explain orbital data
3. **Relationship demonstrations** - Show understanding of key physics relationships
4. **Application scenarios** - Solve real-world orbital mechanics problems

### Mastery Indicators:
- Correct prediction of orbital changes
- Accurate interpretation of orbital parameters
- Explanation of underlying physics principles
- Successful completion of increasingly complex challenges

## 13. Interaction Design
### Core Interactions:
1. **Direct manipulation** - Drag objects to set initial positions/velocities
2. **Parameter adjustment** - Sliders for mass, speed, distance, angle
3. **Time control** - Play, pause, slow motion, reverse time
4. **View manipulation** - Rotate, zoom, pan the 3D view
5. **Data exploration** - Hover for detailed information at points

### Learning-Focused Interactions:
1. **Guided tutorials** - Step-by-step introduction to concepts
2. **Discovery challenges** - Open-ended exploration with prompts
3. **What-if scenarios** - Systematic variation of parameters
4. **Error correction** - Identify and fix unstable orbits
5. **Comparison mode** - Multiple orbits simultaneously

### Interaction Flow:
- **Explore → Predict → Manipulate → Observe → Reflect**
- **Simple → Complex → Synthesis → Application**
